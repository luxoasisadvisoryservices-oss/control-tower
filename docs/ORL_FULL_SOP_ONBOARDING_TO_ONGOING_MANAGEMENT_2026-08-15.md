## Core Principle

ORL is not a pricing-button service. It is a supervised revenue-management operating system.

The pricing tool gives data and automation. Damiano's role is to turn that into strategy:

1. Diagnose the real problem before lowering rates.
2. Know whether the issue is price, demand, visibility, restrictions, listing quality, owner constraints, channel setup or missing data.
3. Run a repeatable cadence: forecast, optimise, control, monitor.
4. Protect revenue in peak periods and stimulate demand intelligently in shoulder/low periods.
5. Communicate strategy before numbers create owner anxiety.
6. Keep all changes advisory or approval-gated unless a written scope says otherwise.

## Non-Negotiables

1. No pricing work starts until the listing identity is confirmed.
2. No managed implementation starts until contract, payment/start date and access are clear.
3. Do not judge a strategy from one or two empty days.
4. Do not rely on PriceLabs alone. Check PMS, OTA channels, listing visibility, fees, restrictions and owner constraints.
5. Do not make emotional daily overrides unless there is a real demand signal or error.
6. Do not use old Lux Oasis Holiday Homes branding for ORL client work. Use Oasis Revenue Lab / Lux Oasis Advisory & Services LLC.
7. Do not expose bot, server, API or internal setup details in client-facing messages.
8. Do not change prices, listings, cancellation policies, promotions or external content without the approved client authority.

## Part 3: Discovery Call SOP

### Pre-Call Preparation

Before the call:

1. Review questionnaire.
2. Review calculator.
3. Open public listing links as a guest.
4. Check whether PriceLabs exists and listings are connected.
5. Identify possible listing keywords and property clusters.
6. Note missing data.
7. Prepare the first diagnosis, but do not finalise recommendations yet.

### Call Structure

Use selectively, not as an interrogation.

1. **Context**
   - What made you feel you needed help with pricing now?
   - If nothing changes in the next 3 months, what worries you most?

2. **Goal validation**
   - What does a successful month mean: revenue, occupancy or both?
   - Is that goal realistic for the market and property?

3. **Risk calibration**
   - Are you more afraid of empty nights or underpricing booked nights?
   - In a 30-day month, would you prefer 3-5 empty days at higher ADR or a full calendar at lower prices?

4. **PriceLabs depth**
   - Who set up PriceLabs?
   - Was base price adjusted using real performance data?
   - Is the minimum price trusted or guessed?

5. **OTA visibility**
   - Do you track listing position against competitors?
   - Have bookings slowed even when prices look competitive?
   - Are you open to not panicking early on empty days if it helps attract higher-paying guests?

6. **Expectation alignment**
   - When prices rise and bookings slow temporarily, can you hold strategy instead of reacting?
   - Do you prefer weekly decisions based on data or daily decisions based on emotion?

7. **Decision framing**
   - If ORL shows where revenue is leaking, do they want one-time optimisation, a managed trial period or ongoing support?

### Call Outcome

By the end of the call, confirm:

1. Client goal.
2. Risk profile.
3. Property/listing scope.
4. Cost floor.
5. Access still needed.
6. Whether ORL is doing:
   - audit only
   - setup only
   - ongoing revenue management
   - premium visibility/channel diagnosis
7. Next step: agreement/payment/setup or data request.

## Part 5: Initial Revenue Setup

### Cost Floor And Targets

Use the calculator to define:

1. Break-even nightly rate.
2. Minimum acceptable floor.
3. Target nights.
4. Target revenue.
5. Buffer percentage.

Dubai guide:

1. JBR / Marina: 24-26 target nights, around 78-85%.
2. Downtown: 22-24 target nights, around 70-77%.
3. New listing: start around 20 target nights until reviews build.
4. Standard buffer: 15%.
5. New listing buffer: 20%.
6. Aggressive/high-competition buffer: 10%.

## Part 6: Website AI-Readiness SOP

### When To Run

Run at onboarding for any client with a direct-booking website.

Re-run:

1. After website changes.
2. Quarterly.
3. Before a direct-booking growth push.
4. When owner/client asks why direct bookings are weak.

### How To Run

Use Google Rich Results Test:

`https://search.google.com/test/rich-results`

Test:

1. Homepage.
2. Best listing page.
3. Weak listing page.
4. Priority-growth listing page.

Score:

1. Pass: valid hospitality/listing schema detected.
2. Partial: generic schema but weak listing/vacation-rental structure.
3. Fail: no items detected.

Required schema signals:

1. Organisation.
2. Local business.
3. Vacation rental/lodging.
4. Accommodation/place.
5. Address and geo.
6. Listing name and description.
7. Bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy.
8. Amenities.
9. Images.
10. Reviews/ratings where real and visible.
11. Breadcrumbs.
12. FAQ where visible.
13. Offers/price range where appropriate.

Website manager request:

```text
Please implement structured schema markup on the homepage and listing pages so Google and AI travel-planning tools can clearly understand that these are short-term-rental / vacation-rental listing pages.

Include organisation, local business/lodging, property location, listing description, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy, amenities, images and real review/rating snippets where visible.

After implementation, please send Google Rich Results Test links or screenshots showing the valid items detected.
```

## Part 7: Revenue Diagnosis Framework

When performance is weak, do not start by lowering prices.

Diagnose in this order:

1. Goal
   - Is the goal clear and realistic?

2. Data accuracy
   - Are PMS, PriceLabs, OTA and cost data correct?

3. Market demand
   - Is the market down?
   - Are demand/search signals weak?

4. Booking pace
   - Pace versus last year.
   - Pace versus target.

5. Price position
   - Price versus comp set.
   - ADR versus market.
   - Floor/ceiling logic.

6. Visibility
   - Search rank.
   - Listing position.
   - OTA merchandising.
   - Direct website schema/crawlability.

7. Conversion
   - Photos.
   - Title.
   - Description.
   - Amenities.
   - Reviews.
   - Trust.

8. Restrictions
   - Minimum stay.
   - Cancellation policy.
   - Check-in days.
   - Blocked dates.
   - Availability gaps.

9. Channel setup
   - Markups.
   - Price parity.
   - OTA fees.
   - PMS integration behaviour.

10. Owner constraints
   - Hard minimums.
   - Blocked use.
   - Unrealistic expectations.

11. Operational issues
   - Reviews, cleaning, maintenance, access, guest issues.

Only after this diagnosis decide whether the action is price, listing, restriction, channel, owner conversation, website fix or no action.

## Part 8: Cadence

### Daily Check

Use for active managed clients and Lux Oasis units.

Check:

1. Pickup since yesterday.
2. New bookings.
3. Cancellations.
4. Next 7 days gaps.
5. Same-week orphan nights.
6. Critical event dates.
7. Rate anomalies.
8. Minimum-stay problems on near dates.
9. PMS/channel sync errors if visible.
10. Guest/ops issues that can affect reviews or availability.

Do not check obsessively:

1. Do not rewrite strategy daily from emotion.
2. Do not lower price just because one day is empty.
3. Do not manually override every small PriceLabs movement.
4. Do not chase vanity occupancy if ADR/net revenue suffers.

Output:

- short internal note or Telegram brief only if something needs action.

### Three Times Per Week

Suggested days: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

Check:

1. Next 14 days pickup.
2. Next 30 days pickup.
3. Gaps and orphan nights.
4. Last-minute discount behaviour.
5. Competitor movement for priority listings.
6. OTA visibility/rank where tracked.
7. Website/direct-booking enquiry signal if relevant.
8. Owner/client questions waiting.

Action:

1. Small tactical adjustments if approved/in scope.
2. Draft approval card if external/pricing action needs client approval.
3. Record watch items.

### Weekly Revenue Review

Check:

1. Booking pace versus last year.
2. Booking pace versus current target.
3. Occupancy by window: 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 days.
4. ADR versus target.
5. Revenue versus floor and target.
6. Market demand and events.
7. Comp-set pricing.
8. Restrictions.
9. Cancellation policy fit.
10. Listing conversion/visibility.
11. Owner constraints.
12. Whether strategy still matches risk profile.

Output:

1. Ahead / on track / behind.
2. Main reason.
3. One action.
4. One watch item.
5. One data/client question if needed.

### Monthly Review

Run at month close.

Check:

1. Closed-month occupancy.
2. ADR.
3. Revenue.
4. RevPAR where useful.
5. Net versus gross.
6. Cost-floor performance.
7. Target achievement.
8. Year-on-year where data exists.
9. Market comparison.
10. Listing/channel issues.
11. Strategy changes made.
12. Next month plan.

Output:

- client/owner PDF or written report.

Monthly report must answer:

`What happened, why it happened, what we changed, and what we do next.`

### Quarterly Review

Check:

1. Updated cost base.
2. Rent/owner cost changes.
3. Cleaning cost changes.
4. OTA fee/VAT/tax changes.
5. Price floor still valid.
6. Comp set still correct.
7. Property cluster still correct.
8. Website schema still passes.
9. Listing content still competitive.
10. Reviews/reputation trend.
11. Commercial fee/scope still makes sense.
12. Client goal still current.

Output:

- refreshed strategy and next-quarter focus.

### Seasonal / Peak-Season Review

Start before the market does.

For seasonal markets, peak-season strategy starts months before first peak guest arrives.

At 90 days before peak:

1. Cluster properties.
2. Audit remaining inventory.
3. Audit pricing.
4. Audit restrictions.
5. Audit cancellation policy.
6. Divide calendar into:
   - days to protect
   - days to push
   - days needing immediate action
7. Check booking pace versus last year and target.
8. Check search activity.
9. Check lead time.
10. Check how quickly best dates are filling.

Peak season:

1. Protect rate.
2. Protect availability.
3. Avoid selling best dates too cheaply or too early.
4. Use stricter cancellation policies farther out.
5. Avoid short-notice cancellation exposure on high-value dates.

Shoulder season:

1. Stimulate demand intelligently.
2. Use longer-stay discounts where useful.
3. Use flexible cancellation where appropriate.
4. Offer value adds: early check-in, late checkout, luggage storage where operationally possible.
5. Reposition to different guest segments.

Low season:

1. Consider monthly/transient/mid-term stays.
2. Adjust guest segment.
3. Lower strategy only with floor awareness.
4. Use downtime to fix SOPs, listing quality, website schema and owner reporting.

## Part 10: Owner / Client Communication

Principle:

`Explain the strategy before the numbers create anxiety.`

Owner/client does not need every data point. They need:

1. What is happening.
2. Why it is happening.
3. What ORL is doing.
4. What will be done next.
5. When the result should be judged.

Rules:

1. Do not wait for the owner to panic.
2. Do not over-report every normal fluctuation.
3. Do not assess seasonal performance too early.
4. For seasonal markets, final assessment should be at season end, with interim pace updates.
5. Use one consistent communication person where possible.

## Part 11: What To Check And What Not To Check

### Always Check

1. Goal.
2. Cost floor.
3. Booking pace versus last year.
4. Booking pace versus target.
5. Search/demand signal.
6. Lead time.
7. Best dates filling speed.
8. Remaining inventory.
9. Price versus comp set.
10. Minimum-stay rules.
11. Cancellation policy.
12. OTA visibility/listing position.
13. Listing conversion.
14. Channel markups.
15. Website schema/AI-readiness for direct-booking sites.
16. Owner constraints.
17. Data accuracy.

### Do Not Over-Check

1. Do not obsess over one empty day.
2. Do not chase 100% occupancy by default.
3. Do not compare to bad comps.
4. Do not trust market data without checking if the comp set is clean.
5. Do not check only PriceLabs and ignore PMS/OTA/channel reality.
6. Do not judge peak-season strategy before enough pace data exists.
7. Do not use one signal alone. Booking pace, search activity, lead time and best-date fill speed should be read together.
8. Do not make owner promises from unverified data.
9. Do not let a client pressure ORL into recommendations before configuration is complete.
10. Do not apply one strategy across all seasons.

### Audit Package Rule

Separate internal workbooks from client-facing deliverables.

Internal workbook can contain:

1. uncertain public-source mapping.
2. provisional hypotheses.
3. weak competitor sets.
4. internal commercial notes.
5. missing-data warnings.
6. implementation prompts.

Client-facing report must contain:

1. confirmed facts.
2. clearly labelled assumptions.
3. practical recommendations.
4. no internal uncertainty disguised as confidence.
5. no mention that public search/scraping was used unless appropriate.
6. no exact ADR/revenue claim unless supported by confirmed data.

### Audit Package Step 1: Portfolio Map

Build one row per listing, not one row per physical property.

Fields:

1. Cluster.
2. Listing name.
3. Platform.
4. Product type.
5. Capacity.
6. Rating/reviews.
7. Public link.
8. Host role.
9. Booking.com counterpart.
10. Airbnb counterpart.
11. Physical unit mapping status.
12. Calendar overlap risk.
13. Priority.
14. Risk.

Special warning:

If a client has both whole-unit listings and room-level listings, do not price before mapping calendar relationships. Room inventory can cannibalise whole-unit value if calendar logic is wrong.

### Audit Package Step 2: Cluster Strategy

Do not use one strategy for a whole portfolio.

Cluster by:

1. micro-market.
2. product type.
3. capacity.
4. guest segment.
5. seasonality.
6. review strength.
7. whole-unit versus room-split model.
8. ownership/lease/management model.

For each cluster, define:

1. base strategy.
2. peak protection.
3. shoulder-season stimulation.
4. low-season alternative.
5. minimum-stay logic.
6. cancellation-policy logic.
7. photo/listing priority.
8. data still needed.

### Audit Package Step 3: Unit Strategy

For every unit/listing, write:

1. immediate improvement needed.
2. revenue / PriceLabs strategy.
3. competitor focus.
4. priority.
5. risk.

Examples of improvement categories:

1. first photo / money shot.
2. title clarity.
3. description value.
4. sleeping-plan clarity.
5. bathroom/amenity constraint.
6. location explanation.
7. review/reputation issue.
8. room/shared-space ambiguity.
9. far-out pricing too low.
10. minimum-stay fragmentation.
11. calendar cannibalisation.

### Audit Package Step 4: Competitor Verification

Competitor sets must be verified before pricing decisions.

For every target listing, classify each competitor as:

1. like-for-like.
2. ceiling reference.
3. value reference.
4. amenity reference.
5. ramp/reference only.

Only like-for-like competitors can drive base price decisions.

Competitor verification fields:

1. target listing.
2. target platform.
3. micro-market.
4. product type.
5. competitor name.
6. competitor platform.
7. URL.
8. same platform as target.
9. role in set.
10. capacity.
11. bedroom/bathroom count.
12. quality signal.
13. review count.
14. location strength.
15. visible price sample where legally/ethically available.
16. minimum stay.
17. cancellation policy.
18. gap/availability signal.
19. notes.

Weak-set flags:

1. zero like-for-like competitors.
2. no same-platform competitors.
3. wrong micro-market.
4. room inventory compared to whole apartments.
5. old or unverifiable URLs.
6. public snippets but no live listing proof.

Action when weak:

`Do not make ADR/base-price decisions from this set. Find 3+ real like-for-like competitors first.`

6. cleaning fees.
7. OTA commissions.

### Audit Package Step 6: Client-Safe Rules

Use these rules in every client-facing audit:

1. Do not invent exact ADR, occupancy, revenue or competitor prices.
2. Mark public data as provisional until official links and exports are confirmed.
3. Keep internal commercial notes out of the client version.
4. Do not tell the client to raise prices simply because reviews are strong.
5. Strong reviews are a signal, not a pricing decision.
6. Connect every recommendation to confirmed data or a clearly labelled hypothesis.
7. If the dataset is incomplete, recommend a controlled pilot rather than a portfolio-wide reset.

### Audit Package Step 7: Copy And Title Upgrades

Title standard:

1. lead with the strongest selling point.
2. include location/micro-market.
3. include capacity/use case where useful.
4. avoid vague emotional titles without search value.
5. avoid clutter.

Copy upgrade checks:

3. location friction is explained.
4. guest segment is obvious.
5. shared/private spaces are clear.
6. parking, beach distance, public transport, lift, AC, workspace and bathrooms are explicit where relevant.
7. no amenities are over-promised.

10. public-link uncertainty.

4. low.

### Audit Package Step 9: First 30-Day Pilot

When data is incomplete or portfolio is complex, do not reset everything.

Run a 30-day pilot on the most valuable/urgent clusters.

Week 1:

1. confirm mapping.
2. clean comp set.
3. validate floor.
4. verify PriceLabs setup.
5. identify restrictions.

Week 2:

1. implement approved setup changes.
2. fix title/photos/copy issues in scope.
3. set watch dates.
4. set owner communication.

Week 3:

1. monitor pace.
2. compare pickup.
3. inspect gaps and restrictions.
4. adjust only where evidence supports it.

Week 4:

1. summarise result.
2. decide scale, revise or pause.
3. create next 30-day plan.

## Final Operating Rule

The best revenue manager is not the one who touches prices the most.

1. when to protect rate,
2. when to stimulate demand,
3. when the problem is not price,
4. when the owner needs explanation before anxiety,
5. when software should act,
