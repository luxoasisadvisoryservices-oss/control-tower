## Qualification Rules

Primary target hosts:

- Rome and Milan first.
- Individual hosts, co-host pairs or small founder-led operators with several Airbnb listings in strong locations.
- Best pattern: 2-10 listings in Rome or Milan. Good reviews, attractive apartments and good locations, but not a polished professional revenue-management operation.
- 5 properties, 3 properties, 2 properties or up to around 10 properties are all acceptable when the operator still feels reachable and not too corporate.
- Prioritise hosts who are doing a good job already but have visible upside: bad or average cover photo, poor photo order, weak title, unclear description, weak amenity presentation, messy guest journey, calendar gaps, static pricing, flat discounts or no visible dynamic-pricing discipline.
- Static pricing is a core signal: flat nightly rates across dates, weak weekend/event/seasonality variation, stale discounts, no visible PriceLabs-like logic, or pricing that looks manual rather than responsive.
- The target is not "bad hosts". The target is capable small hosts with clear potential: good location and guest demand, but still possible to improve ADR, occupancy, RevPAR and conversion with PriceLabs, listing optimisation and calendar strategy.

Avoid:

- Hotels.
- Large hotel chains.
- Big public property-management companies unless kept only as market-map references.
- Operators that already look highly systemised, with strong pricing tech and polished listings, unless there is a very specific audit angle.
- Generic leads without public contact evidence.
- Identical outreach or invented contact details.

Do not make the first outreach batch from big public companies. The ideal target is closer to Damiano's Casa di Testaccio example: a small host/operator with good locations and reviews, but presentation and pricing gaps that make a personalised Revenue Management/listing audit credible.
