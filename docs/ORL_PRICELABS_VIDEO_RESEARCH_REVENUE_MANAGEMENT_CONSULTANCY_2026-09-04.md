## Executive Takeaway

For Oasis Revenue Lab, the sellable service should not be "I set up PriceLabs". The stronger consultancy product is:

1. Better decisions from better data.
2. Weekly revenue meetings that end with actions.
3. Owner communication before owners panic.
4. Market-aware pricing, not blind last-year comparisons.
5. Cross-team alignment between pricing, marketing, distribution and operations.
6. Protection from accidental profit leaks such as stacked discounts, OTA promos, fees and poor minimum-stay logic.

The practical positioning: "We do not just change nightly prices. We review the commercial system around the listing and tell you what to do next."

## What Matters From Each Video

### 1. Better Data, Not Better Tools

Main point: once everyone has access to dynamic pricing, PMSs, OTAs and dashboards, the advantage is not the tool. The advantage is decision quality.

Use for consultancy:

- Sell interpretation and action, not dashboards.
- Ask whether each analysis drives a specific action, decision or KPI.
- Do not build custom tech unless it creates real differentiation; buy/rent generic tools and build only what creates a proprietary edge.
- Owned demand matters. OTA demand is rented demand; direct repeat demand compounds.
- Supply quality matters. Revenue management is downstream of inventory quality, owner retention and guest conversion.
- AI is useful because it reduces the cost of analysis and pushes insight to the people who can act.

Client-facing line:

> Most hosts do not have a data problem. They have a decision problem. Our job is to turn pricing, market and listing data into the next correct action.

### 2. RM Mastery Lab - Navigating a Shifting Market

Main point: soft markets and shorter booking windows require calm diagnosis, not panic discounting.

Useful points:

- Revenue management cannot work in a silo. The RM must understand operations, reviews, guest communication and property-level behaviour.
- Dubai was discussed as a short-booking-window market where performance can shift quickly.
- In soft demand, owners need education early because they compare current performance with unrealistic peak periods.
- The consultancy value is knowing the portfolio personally and explaining why a property is reacting differently from the market.
- PriceLabs partner updates mentioned: RM partner-level access, pricing profiles, subgroups, Guesty check-in/check-out availability, training hub and neighbourhood data inside pricing calendars.

Use for Oasis Revenue Lab:

- Build a short-booking-window diagnostic for Dubai and seasonal markets.
- Put "owner explanation" into every report: what changed, why it changed, what action we are taking.
- Do not simply reduce price when demand softens. Check booking window, minimum stay, listing quality, reviews, channel visibility and competitor pressure first.

### 3. Shoulder Season Is Coming

Main point: in shoulder season, price is the output, not the input.

Useful points:

- Shoulder season travellers are not only bargain hunters. They can include flexible-date guests, remote workers, longer-stay travellers and people avoiding peak-season crowds.
- Teams often react by lowering prices, but pricing alone misses the real levers.
- Discounts, OTA promotions, long-stay discounts, fees, sponsored listings and marketing offers can stack together and destroy profit.
- Revenue, marketing, distribution and operations must be aligned before shoulder season starts.
- Operations has revenue information: maintenance holds, cleanliness/review issues, access problems, property quality and owner blocks all affect bookability.
- Owner communication matters because shoulder season is also switching season, when owners start comparing managers.

Use for Oasis Revenue Lab:

- Build a "discount stack audit" into client onboarding.
- Check whether a client has long-stay discount plus OTA promo plus platform programme plus manual price cuts.
- During softer periods, recommend traveller-type strategies: midweek flexible offers, long-stay rules, remote-worker positioning, direct-booking messages and channel-specific promotion controls.

Client-facing line:

> Before we lower the price, we check whether the listing is being made expensive, invisible or unprofitable by settings around the price.

### 4. Weekly Revenue Meetings

Main point: a revenue meeting is not for reading dashboards. It is for deciding what will change next.

Useful points:

- Start top-down, then go bottom-up.
- Top-down: total portfolio health, rolling 12-month view, market comparison, demand shifts and supply changes.
- Bottom-up: strongest units, weak units, watchlist units, property-specific blockers.
- Compare to market first. Last year and budget can mislead if the market changed.
- Beating the market can still be a win even when absolute revenue is down.
- Include operations, marketing, distribution and reservations when their decisions affect revenue.
- Key metrics mentioned: ADR, occupancy, RevPAR, available/bookable nights, revenue, market comparison, same-time-last-year context.
- Available nights/bookable nights are critical because owner blocks, maintenance holds and renovation downtime can make revenue targets impossible.

Use for Oasis Revenue Lab:

- Make a weekly revenue meeting framework part of the paid consultancy.
- Every weekly call should end with a decision list: price action, minimum-stay action, promotion/discount action, channel/visibility action, listing/content/review action, and owner/operations blocker action.

## Practical Weekly Meeting Template

1. Market health: demand, supply, booking window and competitor movement.
2. Portfolio health: ADR, occupancy, RevPAR, revenue and bookable nights.
3. Pacing: compare to market first, then last year and budget.
4. Watchlist: units behind market, low conversion, operations blockers or owner holds.
5. Pricing actions: base price, min price, seasonality, last-minute settings and far-out settings.
6. Stay-rule actions: minimum stay, gap nights and check-in/check-out restrictions.
7. Promotion actions: OTA promos, long-stay discounts, mobile/early-bird/last-minute offers and sponsored listings.
8. Listing actions: photos, title, amenities, review score, description and direct-booking angle.
9. Owner communication: what needs to be explained before the owner complains.
10. Decisions: who does what by when.

## Client Red Flags To Diagnose

- Owner says "last year was better" without market comparison.
- Occupancy is down but booking window has shortened.
- Price was reduced but minimum stays still block demand.
- OTA promotion, long-stay discount and manual price cut are active together.
- Marketing pushes offers without checking pricing margin.
- Operations has maintenance holds or owner blocks not reflected in revenue targets.
- The client is paying for demand through OTAs but has no owned-demand/direct-booking strategy.
- Dashboard reviews happen, but no decision is made.
